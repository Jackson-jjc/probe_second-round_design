| **01 · Ask About a Detail**Choose part of an object image. Say what you notice. We also ask when the person checks or rejects the answer.<br> | A connection to an object can come from its history, appearance or a personal memory. These are different reasons. We keep them separate when reading the visitor’s account. This uses relational authenticity and the difference between looking alike and having a real source link. We ask what detail mattered and why. The HCI question is whether physical action gives the person useful control over AI’s next response.<br> | Relational authenticity helps us ask how the visitor connects a new result to the source work. Looking similar, knowing the maker’s history and having made something yourself are separate grounds. We do not decide in advance which ground must matter. [1] [2] | **Present gap — what is missing.**The earlier Probe 2 ended with AI writing about a finished fabric action. That made it close to B, which also asks whether AI preserves someone’s meaning. | **03 · Change One Detail**Select an area of an image, tell AI what may change, then compare the changed version with the source.<br>**We collect:** selected area, instruction, actual image and reasons for keeping or rejecting it.<br> | **Which changes still feel connected to the object, and which changes lose that connection?**We need to hear why a change matters. Choosing the prettier picture does not by itself answer this question. The HCI issue is how people set and check limits for generation.<br> | The difference between resemblance and a real source link helps us separate “looks like it” from “still carries something important from it”. We also record personal preference on its own. [2] [5] | **Present gap — what is missing.**In the early Probe 3, a visitor changes a colour or shape. We could learn which image they prefer. We would still need to know why that change matters for this particular object and what future use is being imagined.<br>**What happens in the book case.** Plant Fiction<br>![Book figure screenshot: Plant Fiction: Selfeater. | **04 · Write a Label Together**Early version: two people write a line each and AI joins them into a label. In B, their original words stay visible, a real reply is added and people can keep different views.<br>**We collect:** both original messages, translation, reply, clarification and the final versions each person accepts.<br> | **When AI helps people describe the same object, whose meaning is kept and what gets lost?**A clear shared sentence may leave out a difference that matters. We examine how the two people notice and deal with that difference. They do not have to agree.<br> | Authentication means a process through which an account comes to be accepted. We ask who accepts a museum explanation and on what grounds. Research on AI writing also helps us check whether a person still recognises their voice. [17] [3] | **Present gap — what is missing.**The early Probe 4 asks two people to write a label and lets AI join their words. A single smooth label can hide where they disagree. It also asks both people to be available together.
