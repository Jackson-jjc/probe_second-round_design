# First-stage development test

01 / THE CURRENT FIRST-STAGE TESTTry A and B, then help choose the next directionProtocol basis: Probe Development Survey, ERGO 117392, V1.0, 21 October 2026. Recruitment begins after ethics approval.

10–15 adultsECS staff or students aged 18 or over. No museum or AI expertise is required.

10–15 minutesTry A, then B, about three minutes each. Complete four optional questionnaire questions.

Prepared AI examplesThe researcher checks AI content before sessions and manually switches examples or assembles cards during the test.

One postcard wallAnonymous A and B cards join a local study wall. Optional paper copies contain only the participant’s own card.

Purpose: compare the two low-fidelity prototypes, identify useful improvements and choose which direction to develop further. The website explains the research designs. Participants try the prototypes on study equipment. Museum fieldwork is a later development stage.

### The session, in order

- Read the information sheet, ask questions and tick consent.
- Try A: choose a source detail, arrange materials and compare prepared cuff/bag effects.
- Try B: write original words, choose a prepared image, then read, reply or clarify on a postcard.
- Complete the four optional questions.
- Check drafts for identifying details and confirm submission. Both cards join the wall.
- Choose whether to take printed copies of your own cards, without others’ replies.

### What we keep

- Consent tick and questionnaire answers.
- A: selected source-detail and prepared-image references.
- B: original words, replies, clarifications, source/image references and reply links.
Keep card text and references to existing images. Submitted cards are visible to later participants. Questionnaire answers remain with the research team and authorised audit staff.

### How the test is recorded

Data come from the submitted questionnaire and postcard text and references. A’s physical arrangements are not retained. The study excludes names, signatures, contacts, demographics, photographs, screenshots, audio, video, observation notes and interaction logs.

AI content is prepared before the session. Participant writing is kept out of AI services. The researcher openly explains the manual simulation.

The four questionnaire questions- Which prototype should we develop further for a museum experience? Tick one.
- A: As you arranged materials and compared the source with AI cuff/bag previews, what kept or changed what felt authentic? Why?
- B: How did your words, the AI image and others' replies affect what you expressed or understood across cultural perspectives?
- Why did you choose that option, and what would you improve, including the postcard wall or take-away?
Question 1 options: A, B, Both equally, Neither, Not sure.

How the feedback informs the next designSummarise preferences and reasons, then themes about authenticity, cultural expression, understanding, AI images and improvements. Read B replies alongside the relevant card. Exclude labelled AI starters from participant evidence. Use the findings to choose and refine a direction.

This first stage gathers responses to low-fidelity examples. A later working-AI study can specify how to capture moment-by-moment interaction and evaluate live generation or translation.

Participation and storageParticipants can skip activities or questions, stop, or discard drafts before submission. After anonymous submission, cards and forms cannot be located for removal. Check the cards for identifying details before submission.

The wall runs locally on study equipment without accounts or public access. Lock paper forms and use University-approved secure UK digital storage. Retain data for at least 10 years after collection or publication, whichever is later, then review disposal.
